The Challenge

Disposal is getting harder.
Reuse is the obvious answer.
The gap is just before desalination.

A growing volume problem

Upstream and midstream operators generate enormous daily volumes of produced water that can no longer be quietly absorbed. Disposal wells are filling up, freshwater availability is declining, and the regulatory window for doing nothing is closing faster than most operators planned for.

Where the treatment train fails

Existing infrastructure handles primary oil removal reasonably well. What it leaves behind — residual emulsified oil — is the fraction that passes through and then disrupts desalination membranes, raises fouling risk, and forces operators to slow down or walk away from reuse commitments they needed to make.

The missing confidence layer

Operators, project partners, and infrastructure financiers all need to trust the reuse pathway before they commit capital. Right now, the step just before desalination is where that trust tends to collapse. That is the exact problem Oleava was built to solve.

Quick Answers

Common questions from operators, partners, and investors.

Where does Oleava fit in the treatment train?

After primary oil removal and before desalination — the choke point where residual emulsified oil still creates fouling risk and weakens reuse reliability. Oleava does not replace upstream treatment; it completes it.

Does this require replacing existing infrastructure?

No. The positioning is entirely additive — Oleava inserts a modular polishing step at the critical gap rather than requiring a full-system replacement decision from operators or their capital partners.

What stage is the technology at today?

TRL-6 demo readiness, with a pilot-ready form factor suitable for operator site visits and performance diligence. The modular architecture is designed for the path from single-site pilot to repeatable commercial deployment.

Who should reach out?

Operators exploring produced water reuse pathways, strategic partners evaluating deployment opportunities, and investors focused on industrial water infrastructure. All have a clear and direct entry point here.
